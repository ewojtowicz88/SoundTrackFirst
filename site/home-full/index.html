02 / EVIDENCE

The premise

Music expands imagination.

Every participant receives the same emotional evidence in song.  Listen to the score and write the scenes.

Soundtrack First begins with a fixed piece or sequence of music and asks the writing to answer it: not by explaining lyrics, but by following feeling, rhythm, rupture, memory, and silence.

03 / DNA

The governing rules

With the audio in place. Everything else expresses the vision.

01

The music is selected first.

Before any scene, character, or premise.

02

Its order is fixed.

The sequence becomes the work’s spine.

03

There is no official interpretation.

The score offers evidence, never answers.

04

Each segment entices a personal narrative.

A song becomes a scene, a turn, or a page of pressure.

05

Share the feelings and collaborate to draw the deepest sentiments

The human condition of vision and emotion brings the score to life.

06

The final segment completes the work.

The last note must let the story land.

05 / CUTS

Choose the cut

Eight ways to hear it.

Solo

One writer. One soundtrack. No witness until the final note.

Relay

Writers alternate scenes, handing the story forward without revision.

Blind Premiere

Only the curator knows the full playlist before the work begins.

Common Score

Many writers receive the same soundtrack and make different films.

First Scene

One person supplies the opening; another carries it to the ending.

Festival Cut

Submitted films or screenplays compete from an identical score.

Classroom Cut

A living assignment in structure, interpretation, and collaboration.

Feature Cut

A theatrical-length playlist produces a production-ready screenplay.
