18 / PREMISE

The shared score

Precious Commodities

1991. A senior in college tends bar while playing his own playlist. He was the first guy in town to discover Pearl Jam. As "Alive" plays, patrons ask who the band is. He is flying high — good looking, fit, graduating, and preparing to move to Chicago for a commodities-firm role.

19 / SEQUENCE

9 songs · one sequence · unbound narratives

  1. 01

    Alive — Pearl Jam

    Flying High His final year of college. Behind the bar, "Alive" comes on and several patrons ask who the band is. He loves being the guy who already knows. Graduation, Chicago, money, career, confidence — his future feels enormous.

  2. 02

    Jamming — Bob Marley & The Wailers

    One Last Weekend His friends arrive near closing and turn the end of his shift into an unofficial send-off. Nobody is ready for him to disappear into suits, trading floors, and Chicago. They insist on one more weekend together before everybody scatters.

  3. 03

    The Boys of Summer — The Ataris

    The Old Beach Town The next morning they pile into cars and head back to the beach town of their college summers. Windows down, old stories improve with every retelling. Then he sees a woman he once believed might have been the one.

  4. 04

    Gin and Juice — Snoop Dogg

    Bad Decisions One drink becomes a packed rental house and the night gets ridiculous. Old friends appear. Somebody produces a bicycle. The future commodities trader ends up shirtless, holding a garden hose, and insisting he can clear a lawn chair on two wheels.

  5. 05

    The Final Countdown — Europe

    Chicago Is Waiting Morning is a disaster: his wallet is missing, his clothes are soaked, and his car is somewhere else. He has hours to pull himself together before Chicago. His friends mobilize like a rescue team, rebuilding the previous night one terrible decision at a time.

  6. 06

    I Melt with You — Modern English

    The Quiet Ride The old girlfriend drives him back. For the first time all weekend, nobody is performing. They talk about who they were, what they expected life to become, and what neither ever said. There is still something between them, but neither asks the question.

  7. 07

    Congratulations — Post Malone feat. Quavo

    Exactly What He Wanted Chicago. New suit. New apartment. Trading floor. Handshakes. Money. Everything he chased arrives as imagined. His old friends leave ridiculous congratulatory messages. From the outside, he won. Yet alone above the city, success feels strangely quiet.

  8. 08

    Give It Away — Red Hot Chili Peppers

    The Box Months later he walks through the office carrying a cardboard box. Coworkers assume he was fired. He wasn’t. He quit. Cut to the old college bar: Pearl Jam is playing, friends erupt when he walks in, and someone asks what he will do now. He smiles. "No idea."

  9. 09

    Don’t Let It Pass — Junip

    Precious Commodities Early morning after the reunion, he walks alone through the nearly empty town as night turns to daylight. Salary, title, apartment, and status look different beside the things he almost let pass: time, friendship, youth, love, freedom, and the ability to change direction. He looks back at the old bar once, then keeps walking. Some commodities become more valuable because they cannot be replaced.
