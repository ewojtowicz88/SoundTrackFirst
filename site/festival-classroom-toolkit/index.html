16 / TOOLKIT

Festival & classroom toolkit

A score can hold a room together.

Practical frameworks for screenings, competitions, workshops, and classrooms. Start with a fixed sequence, then let every writer make a different film from it.

17 / CONTEXT

Choose the context

01

Festival Cut

A public-facing brief for filmmakers, writers, and selectors: one score, a defined time window, and many original responses.

  • Fix the soundtrack and submission window before the call opens.
  • Choose the response form: scene, short, treatment, or produced film.
  • Screen the work together without asking for one definitive reading.

02

Classroom Cut

A flexible structure for teaching pacing, interpretation, and story shape without leading every answer.

  • Set a track-to-task rhythm that fits the class length.
  • Invite private writing first, then optional sharing.
  • Use the soundtrack order as the shared structure for reflection.

03

Filmmaker room

A working session for collaborators who want sound to provoke decisions before the script locks in.

  • Listen through once without writing or discussion.
  • Assign moments, scenes, or visual prompts by track.
  • Keep the first responses visible when the group reviews the material.

18 / PLAN

Run the room

A simple session plan, with enough structure to stay alive.

  1. Before

    Send the invitation

    Share the format, the start time, and the rule that the sequence will not change. Do not attach an interpretation to the music.

  2. At the door

    Set the listening agreement

    Ask participants to silence notifications, work from their own response, and reserve discussion until the end.

  3. During

    Protect the order

    Let every track land before moving on. A pause is part of the score, not a gap to fill with instruction.

  4. After

    Hold the premiere

    Read scenes, compare notes, or screen work. Invite people to name what they heard without correcting anyone else.

19 / MATERIALS

Materials desk

Bring the real brief into the room.

This toolkit is designed to sit beside your own confirmed event details: your soundtrack, timing, participant guidance, accessibility plan, venue rules, and submission criteria. Keep those materials specific to your organizers and audience.

The fixed-score brief

State the title of the score, its running order, response form, and any time limit.

Participant note

Set expectations for listening, authorship, sharing, and the optional nature of reading work aloud.

Submission guide

For competitions, publish deadlines, eligibility, file requirements, rights, and selection process from your organizers.

Accessibility sheet

Share the actual access information for your venue or online room, including captions, breaks, and support contacts.
