The formats

Every score is a different set.

Every version begins when the music arrives. What happens after that depends on who is listening, writing, and passing the work forward.

08 / CHOOSE

Before you press play

The rules do not change. The listening situation does.

Choose the format that matches the room, the risk, and the amount of time you can give the score.

Each cut begins the same way: select the music before story, preserve the order, and let each segment claim a narrative.

09 / INDEX

01

Solo

One writer follows one fixed soundtrack from first note to final landing. There is no room to negotiate the score, only the story it releases.

One sitting to several weeks

BEST FOR

Private practice, a short self-assignment, or the first test of the format.

“What does this sequence make inevitable when nobody else has to agree?”

02

Relay

Writers take turns owning a song or movement. Every contribution becomes a fact the next writer carries forward.

One track per writer

BEST FOR

Writing groups, writers’ rooms, or friends who enjoy leaving a difficult handoff.

“What must the next writer inherit from the scene you leave behind?”

03

Blind Premiere

A curator keeps the entire sequence concealed. Participants receive the score or only the next segment owhen it is time to write it.

Live or session by session

BEST FOR

A reveal, a classroom exercise, or any room that enjoys suspense before the opening note.

“What changes when the future of the score is unavailable to you?”

04

Common Score

Everyone begins with the same playlist. The delight is in the distance between the resulting films, stories, scenes, and poems. Music interpretation through storytelling.

Any shared deadline

BEST FOR

Workshops, public prompts, and comparison without competition.

“Which choices does the score invite, and which ones does only your history supply?”

05

First Scene

One writer answers the opening segment and creates the first scene. Another writer receives that scene and follows the score to its conclusion.

Two writers, one handoff

BEST FOR

Collaborators who want a precise hinge between two imaginations.

“What promise does an opening make when someone else must pay it off?”

06

Festival Cut

A shared score becomes the call for entries. Finished work can be screened, read, discussed, or awarded without asking anyone to write the same story.

A published submission window

BEST FOR

Open calls, showcases, and a fair creative challenge.

“What does a shared score reveal about a community’s different instincts?”

07

Classroom Cut

An instructor supplies the score and frames the assignment. Students can work alone, in pairs, or as a relay—then compare how form shaped their choices.

One class to a full term

BEST FOR

Teaching structure, adaptation, interpretation, and collaboration.

“What can a fixed constraint teach that an open prompt cannot?”

08

Feature Cut

A feature-length playlist sets the pace for a sustained narrative. Each track or movement has a structural job, but never a prescribed meaning.

A long-form writing process

BEST FOR

Long-form screenwriting, novels, or a full production concept.

“How can a fixed score carry a story across an entire evening?”

10 / SETUP

A simple starting procedure

Three decisions before the first sentence.

01

Set the cut

Choose the mode before anyone hears the first segment.

02

Set the score

Build or appoint the fixed soundtrack, then lock its sequence.

03

Write forward

Give every segment a narrative unit and begin without an official reading.

11 / START

Ready when the first track is

Pick the cut. Keep the score. Write what you hear.

A soundtrack is not a mood board. It is a sequence of decisions waiting for a story to answer them.
